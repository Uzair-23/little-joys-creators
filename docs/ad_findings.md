## 1. Executive Summary & Honest Framing

This empirical analysis investigates the creative drivers of performance across Little Joys paid ad accounts under rigorous empirical standards:
1. **No causal claims:** All reported relationships are observational ("is associated with"), never causal.
2. **Duplicate-pair noise calibration (context only):** Based on 3 duplicate creative pairs (`LJ-003/059`, `LJ-036/056`, `LJ-073/093`), background platform noise averages **0.49x ROAS** and **0.46 percentage points CVR**. This noise floor is reported strictly as context, not a pass/fail gate.
3. **Strict 50% Outlier Retention Rule:** To prevent mistaking the single outlier `LJ-004` (₹6.59L spend, ₹32.99L revenue, 5.0x ROAS) for creator strategy, a pattern is accepted only if the effect without `LJ-004` maintains the same sign and retains **at least 50%** of the baseline effect.
4. **False Leads Unmasked:** Creative traits carried by `LJ-004`—including `child_led` format, `curiosity_gap` hooks, `protein_intake` messaging, and `child_on_screen=one`—all retain **under 50%** of their ROAS lift without `LJ-004`. They are classified as fragile false leads and logged in `outputs/tables/ad_not_concluded.csv`.
5. **Pattern Overlap & Non-Independence:** Highly overlapping ad sets (e.g. `problem_statement` hooks and `fear_pain_point` drivers share 47.4% Jaccard overlap) reflect correlated choices, not independent creative levers.
6. **The Hindi Attention-vs-Sales Split:** Hindi language creatives generate the strongest top-of-funnel retention (+10.49% Hook Rate lift, 95% CI [+1.82%, +13.20%]), but underperform on conversion (0.79 ROAS vs 1.31x rest). Top-of-funnel attention does not automatically yield bottom-of-funnel revenue.

### Key Insights

#### 1. Attention vs Sales Split: Hindi Language
* `language == Hindi` is a verified top-of-funnel driver: Hook Rate is **38.00% vs 27.51%** for rest of ads (diff: +10.49 percentage points, 95% CI [+1.82%, +13.20%], adjusted $p = 0.040$). Hold Rate is also elevated (27.08% vs 23.95%).
* Crucially, Hindi does not contain `LJ-004`, so without `LJ-004` its relative hook advantage actually expands (+11.02% diff, 105.1% retained).
* **The Sales Reality:** Hindi ads generate a spend-weighted ROAS of only **0.79** (and CVR of 2.05%), substantially below the rest-of-ads benchmark (ROAS 1.74 with LJ-004, 1.31 without LJ-004). Hindi stops the scroll effectively, but requires stronger bottom-of-funnel creative mechanics to convert.

#### 2. Robust Attention Lever: Child in First 3 Seconds
* Having a child visible in the first 3 seconds (`child_in_first_3s == yes`, $n=34$) drives a robust **+8.09 percentage point lift in Hook Rate** (34.60% vs 26.51%, 95% CI [+4.84%, +11.38%], adjusted $p = 0.0016$).
* Without `LJ-004`, the hook lift is completely preserved (+8.53% diff, 105.5% retained).
* However, on bottom-of-funnel conversion, its baseline ROAS lift (+0.90x) collapses to +0.22x without `LJ-004` (only 24.1% retained), proving it is strictly an attention-stage driver.

## 5. Pattern Overlap & Non-Independence

Patterns in observational ad accounts do not occur in isolation. High Jaccard similarity between ad sets indicates that what appears to be separate findings are often collinear executions of the same underlying ads:

* **`hook_type=problem_statement` and `hook_driver=fear_pain_point`:** Jaccard overlap = **0.4737** (9 out of 12 problem statement ads are fear/pain point drivers).
* **`hook_type=problem_statement` and `main_benefit=fussy_eating`:** Jaccard overlap = **0.3529** (6 shared ads).
* **`hook_type=problem_statement` and `brand_first_said=5_to_10s`:** Jaccard overlap = **0.3529** (6 shared ads).
* **`child_on_screen=one` and `child_in_first_3s=yes`:** Share 34 ads; creatives featuring one child almost invariably place that child in the first 3 seconds.
